## THE ABSOLUTE BASICS

### What is Walt Disney World?
Walt Disney World is a **47-square-mile** resort in Orlando, Florida - roughly the size of San Francisco. It includes:
- **4 Theme Parks:** Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Hollywood Studios, Animal Kingdom
- **2 Water Parks:** Typhoon Lagoon, Blizzard Beach
- **25+ Resort Hotels**
- **Disney Springs:** Shopping/dining complex (free entry)

### The 4 Parks at a Glance

| Park | Known For | Best For | Time Needed |
|------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Magic Kingdom** | Cinderella Castle, classic Disney | Everyone, especially first-timers | Full day |
| **EPCOT** | World Showcase, Festivals | Adults, foodies, older kids | Full day |
| **Hollywood Studios** | Star Wars, Toy Story Land | Star Wars fans, thrill seekers | 3/4 to full day |
| **Animal Kingdom** | Pandora, Safari, animals | Animal lovers, Avatar fans | 3/4 day |

### First Trip? Here's What to Prioritize
If this is your first trip, focus on:
1. **Magic Kingdom** - This IS Disney. Don't skip it.
2. **One other park** minimum (Hollywood Studios or Animal Kingdom most popular)
3. **At least 4-5 days** of parks to not feel rushed
4. **One rest day** if possible - Disney is exhausting!

## PLANNING TIMELINE

### How Far in Advance to Plan

| Task | When to Do It |
|------|---------------|
| Set dates & budget | 6-12 months out |
| Book resort hotel | 6-12 months out (or when ready) |
| Buy park tickets | Before dining reservations open |
| Make dining reservations | **60 days** before check-in (resort guests) |
| Make dining reservations | **60 days** before date (off-site guests) |
| Lightning Lane pre-booking | **7 days** before (resort guests) or **3 days** (off-site) |
| Make park reservations | After buying tickets |

### The Most Important Deadlines
1. **60 days out:** Dining reservations open (set an alarm for 5:45am ET!)
2. **7 or 3 days out:** Lightning Lane Multi-Pass pre-booking opens
3. **Day before:** Download My Disney Experience app, confirm everything

## TICKETS EXPLAINED

### Base Tickets vs. Park Hopper

**Base Ticket:**
- Visit ONE park per day
- Less expensive
- Best for: First-timers, families with young kids, those who like to take it slow

**Park Hopper:**
- Visit MULTIPLE parks in one day (after 2pm)
- More expensive
- Best for: Repeat visitors, adults, short trips where you want to see more

### How Many Days?
- **3-4 days:** Very rushed, won't see everything
- **5-6 days:** Good pace, can see most things with a rest day
- **7+ days:** Relaxed pace, repeat favorites, truly experience Disney

**Pro Tip:** Tickets get CHEAPER per day the more days you add. A 5-day ticket isn't much more than a 4-day.

### Where to Buy
- **disneyworld.com** - Direct from Disney
- **Undercover Tourist** - Reputable discount seller
- **Sam's Club / Costco** - Sometimes has deals
- **AVOID:** Third-party sellers, eBay, Craigslist (scam risk)

## PARK RESERVATIONS

### What Are Park Reservations?
Since 2020, you need BOTH a valid ticket AND a park reservation for each day you visit. You cannot just show up.

### How to Make Them
1. Buy tickets first
2. Link tickets to My Disney Experience account
3. Go to "My Reservations" and select your park for each day
4. Park reservations are FREE but required

### Strategy Tips
- **Make them ASAP** after buying tickets
- **Popular parks** (Magic Kingdom, Hollywood Studios) fill up first
- **Park Hopping:** Only need reservation for your FIRST park of the day
- **Can modify** if plans change and availability exists

## WHERE TO STAY

### On-Site (Disney Resort Hotels) vs. Off-Site

**On-Site Pros:**
- **Early Entry** - Enter parks 30 min before general public (HUGE advantage)
- **Extended Evening Hours** - Deluxe resort guests get extra hours at select parks
- **Lightning Lane** booking 7 days out (vs. 3 days for off-site)
- **Free transportation** - Buses, monorail, Skyliner, boats
- **MagicBand+** benefits
- **Theming and immersion** - You're "in the Disney bubble"

**On-Site Cons:**
- More expensive than most off-site options
- Disney transportation can be slow

**Off-Site Pros:**
- Often cheaper
- More space (vacation homes, suites)
- Kitchen facilities usually included
- Points/rewards if using hotel chains

**Off-Site Cons:**
- No Early Entry (this is significant!)
- Lightning Lane booking only 3 days out
- No free Disney transportation
- Less "magical" experience

### Resort Categories

| Category | Price Range | Example Resorts |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Value** | $150-250/night | All-Stars, Pop Century, Art of Animation |
| **Moderate** | $250-400/night | Caribbean Beach, Coronado Springs, Port Orleans |
| **Deluxe** | $400-800+/night | Polynesian, Grand Floridian, Animal Kingdom Lodge |

### First-Timer Recommendation
**Stay on-site if budget allows.** The Early Entry benefit alone is worth it - you'll experience significantly shorter waits on headliner attractions.

If budget is tight, **Pop Century** or **Art of Animation** offer great value with Skyliner access.

## EARLY ENTRY & EXTENDED EVENING HOURS

### Early Entry (All Disney Resort Guests)
- **What:** Enter any park 30 minutes before official opening
- **When:** Every day
- **Who:** All Disney resort hotel guests
- **Why it matters:** This is when wait times are LOWEST - you can knock out 2-3 major attractions before crowds arrive

### Extended Evening Hours (Deluxe Resort Guests Only)
- **What:** Stay in select parks 2 hours after closing
- **When:** Select nights at select parks
- **Who:** Deluxe and Deluxe Villa resort guests only
- **Why it matters:** Very low crowds, extra ride time

## LIGHTNING LANE EXPLAINED (SIMPLE VERSION)

### What is Lightning Lane?
It's Disney's paid skip-the-line system. There are two types:

**Lightning Lane Multi-Pass (LLMP):**
- Costs $15-35+ per person per day (varies by park and date)
- Book up to 3 attractions at a time
- Can book more after using first ones
- Best for: Magic Kingdom, Hollywood Studios

**Lightning Lane Single Pass (LLSP):**
- Pay per ride for the most popular attractions
- $15-25+ per attraction per person
- Best for: Must-do headliners you don't want to wait for

### Do You Need Lightning Lane?
- **If using Early Entry strategically:** You can skip LLMP at some parks
- **If sleeping in or visiting on busy days:** LLMP is very helpful
- **For rides like Tron, Guardians:** LLSP may be worth it

### First-Timer Recommendation
Budget for **LLMP at Magic Kingdom** and **Hollywood Studios** at minimum. These parks have the most value for the pass.

## DINING BASICS

### Dining Reservation Types

**Table Service:**
- Sit-down restaurants with servers
- **Require reservations** (called ADRs - Advance Dining Reservations)
- Book at **60 days** out starting at 6am ET
- Range from casual to fine dining

**Quick Service:**
- Counter service, cafeteria style
- No reservations needed
- Use **Mobile Order** in the app to skip lines!
- Faster and cheaper than table service

**Character Dining:**
- Characters visit your table during meal
- Popular with kids and Disney fans
- VERY hard to get - book at 60 days!
- Examples: Chef Mickey's, Cinderella's Royal Table, Tusker House

### First-Timer Must-Book Restaurants
If you want these, book EXACTLY at 60 days:
- **Cinderella's Royal Table** (inside the castle!)
- **Be Our Guest** (Beast's castle)
- **Ohana** (Polynesian - family style)
- **Chef Mickey's** (character breakfast)
- **Space 220** (EPCOT)

### Can't Get a Reservation?
- **Keep checking** - Cancellations happen constantly
- **Try walk-up** - Lounges often have same menu
- **Check day-of** - Morning of your visit, refresh the app

## FIRST-TIMER MISTAKES TO AVOID

### Planning Mistakes
1. **Not making dining reservations** - Popular spots book instantly
2. **Underestimating the size** - Each park takes a full day; WDW is massive
3. **Not using Early Entry** - This is your best weapon against crowds
4. **Over-scheduling** - Build in flexibility and rest
5. **Skipping the app** - It's essential, not optional

### In-Park Mistakes
1. **Arriving late** - Rope drop is when magic happens
2. **Eating only at peak times** - Eat at 11am or 2pm, not noon
3. **Not using Mobile Order** - Standing in food lines wastes time
4. **Walking back and forth** - Tour in a logical pattern
5. **Waiting for low wait times that never come** - Some rides are always busy

### Budget Mistakes
1. **Not setting a souvenir budget** - It's easy to overspend
2. **Buying everything inside parks** - Disney Springs and off-site are cheaper
3. **Table service every meal** - Mix in quick service to save money AND time
4. **Not bringing snacks** - Pack granola bars, crackers, etc.
5. **Buying ponchos inside parks** - Bring your own for $1 vs. $15

## SAMPLE FIRST TRIP: 5 DAYS

### Day 1: Arrival Day
- Fly in / Drive in
- Check into resort
- Explore your resort, find pools and amenities
- Dinner at Disney Springs (no park ticket needed)
- Early bedtime!

### Day 2: Magic Kingdom
- Rope drop / Early Entry
- Hit Tomorrowland and Fantasyland in morning
- Afternoon break at hotel
- Return for evening, fireworks
- Lightning Lane Multi-Pass recommended

### Day 3: Hollywood Studios
- Rope drop for Rise of the Resistance or Slinky Dog
- Galaxy's Edge exploration
- Toy Story Land
- Fantasmic! if showing
- Lightning Lane Multi-Pass recommended

### Day 4: Rest Day / Disney Springs
- Sleep in!
- Resort pool time
- Disney Springs for lunch and shopping
- Optional: Water park if you have tickets

### Day 5: Animal Kingdom
- Rope drop for Flight of Passage
- Safari in the morning (animals are active)
- Shows in afternoon
- Pandora at night (it glows!)
- Can often skip LLMP with good Early Entry strategy

### Day 6: EPCOT + Departure
- Morning rides (Guardians, Remy, Test Track)
- World Showcase opens at 11am
- Lunch around the world
- Depart for airport
- (Or make this a full day if flying out next morning)

## FIRST-TIMER FAQS

**Q: Which park should I do first?**
A: Magic Kingdom. It's the quintessential Disney experience.

**Q: How early should I arrive at rope drop?**
A: For Early Entry, arrive when parking opens (usually 60-90 min before posted opening). For regular rope drop, 30-45 min before.

**Q: Is the Dining Plan worth it?**
A: Disney Dining Plan (when available) is usually NOT worth it for most guests. You end up overeating to "get your money's worth."

**Q: Can I bring food into the parks?**
A: Yes! Snacks and outside food are allowed. No glass, no alcohol.

**Q: Are strollers allowed?**
A: Yes, and you can rent them if needed. Must be under 31"x52".

**Q: What if it rains?**
A: Keep touring! Lines get shorter, and rain usually passes in 30-60 min. Bring ponchos.

**Q: Can I see all 4 parks in one trip?**
A: You CAN, but should you? For a relaxed first trip, 2-3 parks done well beats 4 parks rushed.

**Q: When is the best time to visit?**
A: Lower crowds: Mid-January to early Feb, late Aug-Sept, early Dec before Christmas week. But "slow" Disney is still busy.

## FINAL FIRST-TIMER TIPS

1. **Make a plan but stay flexible** - Things will go "wrong." Roll with it.
2. **It's not a race** - You can't do everything. That's okay.
3. **Take breaks** - An afternoon pool break prevents meltdowns (for kids AND adults)
4. **Eat breakfast before the parks** - Don't waste morning park time on food
5. **Charge your phone overnight** - And bring a backup battery
6. **The best souvenir is the experience** - You don't need to buy everything
7. **Talk to Cast Members** - They're incredibly helpful and often share magic
8. **Celebrate everything** - First visit buttons, birthday buttons, etc. are free at Guest Relations!
9. **Take photos, but also put the phone down** - Be present in the magic
10. **You WILL want to come back** - Don't try to cram everything into one trip
